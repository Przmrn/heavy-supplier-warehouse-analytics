## 1. Programme overview

### Project introduction

Analyse heavy supplier, product, inventory, and warehouse data to understand and improve supply-chain operations, inventory health, and warehouse efficiency.

Warehouses handling heavy goods face challenges including:

- Stock imbalance.
- Overstocking and stockouts.
- Inefficient use of space.
- Unreliable suppliers.
- Inaccurate demand forecasting.

Raw operational data exists, but structured analysis is needed to turn it into decisions. Build a data-driven warehouse intelligence capability that helps an organisation:

1. Understand product movement and demand patterns.
2. Measure inventory health and utilisation.
3. Evaluate warehouse operational efficiency.
4. Reduce stock risks and operational waste.
5. Support better procurement and planning decisions.

### Problem statement

Warehouse and supply-chain teams often operate reactively, relying on manual reports or intuition to manage inventory, storage, and space. This can lead to excess inventory, frequent stockouts, poor space usage, and delayed decisions.

### Goal

Build a unified analytics framework using historical warehouse and supplier data to analyse, predict, and optimise product movement, inventory levels, and warehouse performance.

### Business value

- Optimise inventory levels and reduce capital locked in stock.
- Identify fast-moving, slow-moving, and critical products.
- Improve warehouse space utilisation and efficiency.
- Detect stock risks and operational anomalies early.
- Support data-driven procurement and replenishment decisions.
- Improve overall warehouse reliability and performance.

The work is relevant to supply-chain and operations teams, warehouse managers, procurement and planning teams, data analysts, and data scientists.

## 2. How the programme works

Participants work on an assigned project in weekly sprints, submit work, meet their team, and have their contributions assessed for certification.

### Weekly rhythm

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Plan | The week's Scrum Master runs a planning meeting. The team agrees on a sprint goal and selects tasks from the catalogue. |
| 2. Build | Each member works on assigned tasks, such as cleaning data, building analyses, dashboards, or models. |
| 3. Collaborate | The team meets for stand-ups or check-ins to share progress, unblock one another, and review work. |
| 4. Submit | The team uploads that week's work to GitHub and pastes the repository link into the portal's Submission area. |
| 5. Review | Work is reviewed for quality and contribution, informing the final certificate and reference. |

## 3. Team structure and collaboration

A team consists of three interns working on the same dataset and business problem.

## 4. Scrum and weekly working model

### Scrum Master rotation

- Each week, one member acts as Scrum Master.
- The role rotates weekly; there is no permanent leader under the model described in the brief.

2. Define the sprint goal together with the team.
3. Break the sprint into tasks and help allocate them.
4. Coordinate and track progress throughout the week.
5. Ensure every member contributes.
6. Upload the sprint notes to GitHub.

Explore the dataset first. Propose your own KPIs and a short list of analysis questions the team finds interesting. Then select tasks that support those questions.

Use the catalogue as a menu of ideas and a coverage checklist. The brief states there is no limit on the number of tasks the team can take on. It allows choosing any number of tasks and mixing analysis with data science. It does not instruct the team to complete every task in the catalogue.

## Evidence and analytical accuracy

- Never invent dataset columns, joins, numeric results, meeting attendance, contributions, or completion status.
- Identify each table's unit of observation and actual key fields before joining. Check that joins do not unexpectedly duplicate or drop records.
- Preserve original data and document preparation decisions. Keep transformed outputs identifiable.
- If data is already clean, record the checks supporting that conclusion rather than introducing unnecessary cleaning.
- Define KPIs with their business purpose, formula, units, time period, required fields, and assumptions.
- Check that available data supports the chosen analysis. For example, do not claim supplier delivery reliability without appropriate delivery and expected-date evidence.
- Keep findings distinct from recommendations and assumptions.
- For predictive work, choose validation appropriate to the problem and time structure, and report performance and limitations honestly.
- Do not promise a certificate, reference, or job recommendation; these depend on CadetX assessment.
